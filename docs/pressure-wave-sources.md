# Pressure and wave-method sources

These primary sources justify Caevera's analysis choices. They do not define
universal acceptance thresholds and they do not prove a diagnostic cause in a
analyzed case.

1. M. D. Bohon, R. Bluemner, C. O. Paschereit, and E. J. Gutmark,
   “Measuring Rotating Detonation Combustion Using Cross-Correlation,” *Flow,
   Turbulence and Combustion*, 103, 271–292 (2019).
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s10494-019-00017-z
   - Used for: windowed cross-correlation, propagation-delay reasoning, and the
     requirement to expose ambiguity in complex modes.

2. K. J. Durkee, A. Panettieri, G. R. Cobb, and J. W. Bennewitz, “On the
   Determination of Rotating Detonation Engine Wave Modes Using Limited Point
   Measurements,” AIAA SciTech 2026.
   https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2026-2036
   - Used for: limited-point mode identifiability, temporal/spatial spectral
     structure, and the warning against equidistant sensor placement.
   - Caevera does not claim to reproduce the paper's 2D-DFT algorithm; it uses a
     separate candidate phase fit.

3. T. Pritschau, A. Karnam, and E. J. Gutmark, “Characterizing Acoustic Modes
   in a Hollow Rotating Detonation Combustor,” *Combustion and Flame*, 275,
   114057 (2025).
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.combustflame.2025.114057
   - Used for: cross-spectral phase across azimuthal high-frequency pressure
     probes and the distinction between a pressure mode and a detonation front.

4. J. R. Tobias, A. K. Agrawal, and D. E. Paxson, “Experimental and
   Computational Analysis of a Rotating Detonation Combustor,” AIAA SciTech
   2022.
   https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2022-1879
   - Used for: multi-observable experiment–CFD validation using dynamic
     pressure, wave speed, and static-pressure evidence.

5. T. Sato et al., “Space Flight of Liquid Rotating Detonation Engine Using
   Sounding Rocket S-520-34,” *Journal of Spacecraft and Rockets* (2025).
   https://doi.org/10.2514/1.A36447
   - Used for: time-local frequency analysis capable of exposing sequential
     deflagration, multi-wave, and single-wave intervals.
